Infectious Diseases

Invasive Group A Streptococcus

Group A Streptococcus (GAS) causes a wide spectrum of disease, from mild pharyngitis to life-threatening invasive infections including necrotizing fasciitis and streptococcal toxic shock syndrome. Rates of invasive GAS have risen substantially since 2017. This dashboard draws on CDC Active Bacterial Core (ABCs) surveillance data spanning 1997–2023.

About this chart
Rates are estimated from CDC Active Bacterial Core (ABCs) surveillance, a population-based active surveillance system that identifies all laboratory-confirmed cases of invasive GAS disease (GAS isolated from a normally sterile site) in defined geographic areas. Rates are calculated per 100,000 population using U.S. Census intercensal and postcensal estimates. Death rates reflect in-hospital deaths among ABCs cases. Geographic coverage varies slightly across years.

About this chart
Each point is one ABCs surveillance state. The horizontal spread within each category is a random jitter added for legibility; the y-axis value (rate per 100,000) is exact. The box spans the interquartile range (IQR) of state rates; the line shows the median. Wider spread indicates greater between-state heterogeneity. New Mexico consistently reports the highest rates across demographic groups, likely driven by higher prevalence of underlying risk conditions (e.g., diabetes, homelessness, substance use disorder).

About this chart
Percent of invasive GAS isolates tested that were non-susceptible to each antibiotic. GAS remains uniformly susceptible to penicillin and vancomycin; those lines are omitted for clarity. Erythromycin and clindamycin resistance are clinically relevant for patients with penicillin allergy.
Data Sources

About the Data

CDC Active Bacterial Core Surveillance (ABCs)
Population-based active laboratory surveillance for invasive bacterial pathogens in 10 U.S. states, covering ~15% of the U.S. population. Cases are defined by isolation of GAS from a normally sterile site (blood, CSF, pleural fluid, etc.). Annual data available 1997–2023. Includes demographic stratification, clinical syndrome, and antimicrobial susceptibility data.
CDC WONDER / Surveillance Reports
Population denominators for rate calculations are derived from U.S. Census Bureau intercensal and postcensal estimates. All incidence rates are per 100,000 persons in the ABCs surveillance area. Data accessed via the PopHIVE ingest pipeline. Public domain — CDC data is not subject to copyright restrictions.

Note: This dashboard uses representative estimates derived from ABCs surveillance data. For clinical or policy decisions, consult the primary CDC surveillance reports. Data are updated annually following ABCs publication.
